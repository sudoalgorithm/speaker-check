Speaker Check

Plays test tones through the speakers, listens with the microphone, and reports dead channels, imbalance, distortion, rattle and frequency gaps.

Before you start

  1. Remove the case. Turn Silent off, Bluetooth off, volume to about 70%.
  2. Put the phone on a table, screen up, in a quiet room. Keep hands off the speaker grilles.
  3. Allow microphone access when asked. Nothing is recorded or uploaded; analysis runs on the phone.
Idle

If the tones come out quietly from the earpiece instead of the main speaker while the mic is on (an iOS routing quirk), use the two-phone mode below with a second device as the microphone.

Result

Frequency response (as heard by the mic)

Level relative to the 1 kHz tone. Phone mics and speakers are not flat, so judge holes and sudden drops, not the overall slope.

Measurements

THD = total harmonic distortion. Rattle = energy that is neither the tone nor its harmonics, relative to the tone; buzzing hardware shows up here.

Manual tone (ear test)

Sweep slowly through 80–400 Hz. A healthy speaker gets quieter at the low end but stays clean; any buzz, crackle or vibrating-part sound is physical damage or debris.

Two-phone mode

More accurate: one phone plays, a second phone listens 10–15 cm away from the speaker under test. Open this page on both.
